# Idea 17: given a physical system, can we understand its function? (working notes, status: idea, not part of the book)

This file is idea 17 of [ideas.qmd](ideas.qmd); the Epilogue points to it from the section *Language, Thought and Translation*. Its sibling, idea 16 (how to embed the meaning of the formal sciences), is in [formal_sciences_meaning.md](formal_sciences_meaning.md). The two ideas share a testbed and a few sources but ask different questions. Iterate here before anything goes into a chapter or the roadmap in `index.qmd`.

Condensed on 2026-11-22: iterations 1 to 4 are summarized in the Background below, and iterations 5 (review against the sources) and 6 (the first experiment) are kept in full. Start with the summary.

## Current state (2026-11-17)

- **The question is old.** Ashby (1956, chapter 6, "The Black Box") posed it with a box "that has just fallen from a Flying Saucer" and answered that experiments identify a mechanism only "up to an isomorphism"; his example is a mechanical oscillator that no test can tell from an RLC circuit. Jonas and Kording (2017) gave the modern version and proposed the processor as a testbed for methods that do not exist yet.
- **What is understanding.** Three formal answers are in hand: isomorphism of the canonical representation (Ashby), causal abstraction that commutes with interventions (Beckers and Halpern; Geiger et al.), and minimal interventions that reach a target behavior (Gurushankar and Grover 2021, undecidable in general by reduction to Rice's theorem). The idea should choose among them.
- **Limits, in two families.** Limits of finite or passive data, which access relaxes (Moore; Gold on positive data; nonlinear ICA and Locatello et al. for a learner that only observes; the causal hierarchy). Limits of equivalence, which access does not relax (Ashby's isomorphism; non-unique explanations, Meloux et al. 2025 and Marom et al. 2009; Rice).
- **What is new here.** Not the testbed idea (Jonas and Kording) nor benchmarks with known ground truth and budgeted experiments (Geng et al. 2025; LUCA 2026). New: observation without the domain's variables, a ladder of heterogeneous systems, module discovery, scoring against an equivalence class, and the axis of access (passive, intervention, replay, trace).
- **First experiment** (iteration 6). A functional autoencoder, trained without labels to predict how a system answers new inputs, on mechanical and electrical oscillators isomorphic by construction (Ashby) and a matched chemical-electrical nonlinear pair; then a contrastive stage that pairs one family across substrates and tests transfer to families never paired. The literature shows generalization within a family and, with a shared prior and a few examples, across related laws; nothing found on contrastive pairing across substrates.

### The testbed: a simulator that hides the domain vocabulary

SPICE exposes voltage and current, VHDL logic levels, Modelica typed ports: each hands the learner the variables, the units and the component vocabulary, so the hardest part of understanding (what to measure, where the module edges are) is given away, and a learner trained on SPICE traces has learned SPICE, not circuits. The requirement is a simulator whose raw observables carry no domain semantics (uniform, low-level, every probe free), so that the learner has to derive voltage, force or logic level. No single simulator is both universal and affordable. The design that survived:

1. **Two layers.** Build systems in a rich domain simulator (SPICE, rigid bodies, an ODE integrator), then expose only a neutral interface: anonymous time series with names and units removed.
2. **Domain-randomized relabeling.** Several simulators, with random linear mixing of the observables, so that only structure transfers. If an embedding puts a mechanical oscillator and an RLC oscillator together despite different observables, it found something independent of the domain.
3. **A cellular automaton as the cleanest first sandbox** (Game of Life, Wireworld and Rule 110 are universal, every cell is probe-able, the designer's hierarchy is known), with the catch that the embedding is a compilation whose scheme is itself a vocabulary, and that a CA has no energy or continuity. Hence several different compilations of the same function, and at least one continuous substrate (Lenia, lattice Boltzmann, or an ODE system).
4. **Candidate substrates** for the second layer, none tested: continuum mechanics and FEM (MOOSE, Elmer, FEniCS), rigid bodies (MuJoCo, Brax, Genesis, NVIDIA Warp), a particle method (MPM in Taichi, solids and fluids in one framework), and bond graphs or port-Hamiltonian systems, which are domain-independent but already carry the prior of effort and flow, so they suit the answer representation and not the observation.
5. **Ground truth** is the designer's hierarchy, recorded when the system is built; the gap between a learner given neutral observables and one given SPICE-style signals measures how much understanding the vocabulary supplies.

### The task: what a method would have to do

Given an unknown system with full probing access: (1) find the boundary (inputs, outputs, state); (2) find the modules (weakly coupled parts with localized interventions); (3) find each module's function, as an input-output description mapped to a known primitive; (4) find the composition; (5) find the purpose, the least determined step, which needs a prior over what systems are for. Candidate approaches: active experimental design (the gap of Jonas and Kording's methods, which were passive); program synthesis and automata learning (L*); causal abstraction, a precise test that intervening on the high-level model predicts intervening on the low level; learned structure with symbolic read-out (SINDy, symbolic regression); and mechanistic interpretability, the same problem with the weights as the netlist. Success, proposed: the recovered hierarchy aligns with the designer's, scored against the class of faithful explanations, and supports repair and counterfactual tests. Not known whether any of this works on the 6502: Jonas and Kording is a negative result for passive standard methods and not a test of better ones (iteration 5, A1).

### The limits, in two families (iteration 5, C)

*Limits of finite or passive data, which access relaxes:* Moore (no finite input-output experiment fixes a finite-state machine; it bounds the state space from below only), Gold (regular languages are not identifiable from positive data; an informant, and with perfect computational traces all recursively enumerable languages, are, Peng et al. 2026), nonlinear ICA (Hyvärinen and Pajunen 1999; restored with an auxiliary variable by Khemakhem et al.), Locatello et al. (i.i.d. observation only), and the causal hierarchy (Bareinboim et al., Ibeling and Icard; a resettable deterministic simulator gives the counterfactual layer directly, which no experiment on an animal can). *Limits that access does not relax:* equivalence up to isomorphism (Ashby 1956), non-unique explanations (Meloux et al. 2025; Marom et al. 2009, a wrong induction with unlimited access), and undecidability for unrestricted classes (Rice; Gurushankar and Grover 2021 for one formalization). No free lunch is a weaker warning than it looks (Goldblum et al.: real problems are low-complexity, so a simplicity bias serves many).

Consequences for an experiment, kept: state the equivalence relation that defines "same function" (behavioral equivalence, bisimulation, equivalence under interventions on declared ports) and score against the class; include negative controls (different structure, identical behavior under the allowed probes, where a correct learner must report ambiguity); state each method's prior (simplicity, modularity, sparsity, independence of mechanisms) and run it where the prior holds and where it fails; report the probe budget and the access level (passive, intervention, replay, trace); treat a found explanation as a hypothesis for the class.

## Iteration 5 (2026-11-17): review against the sources

A review of iterations 1 to 4. The sources below are now held in `references/` (citekeys in `references.bib`, block "Ideas 16 and 17") and were checked with `ground_check.py` and by reading the pages named. Earlier iterations are left as written; this section corrects them where the sources disagree. Not held: Moore 1956 (read through Fields 2013), Gold 1967 (publisher download blocked; read through Peng et al. 2026), Lazebnik 2002, Bongard & Lipson 2007 (abstract only), Simon 1962, Feinberg 2019.

### A. Corrections to earlier iterations

1. **Jonas & Kording did not probe the chip actively.** "Our behavioral mechanisms are entirely passive"; they "just simulated booting" three games, and the only interventions were single-transistor lesions (`jonas2017could`, pp. 13 and 16 of the PLoS version). So iteration 4 B.3 ("the proxy problem sits at the favorable end ... failure there is not a data limit (Jonas & Kording's point)") overstates what they tested. Their point is about the *amount* of data ("the availability of unlimited data ... is in no way sufficient", p. 2), not about the kind of access. Whether active probing would succeed on the 6502 is open, and they say so: "Tasks that isolate one kind of computation would be needed" (p. 16).
2. **The proxy problem is their own proposal.** "It may be an important intermediate step for neuroscience to develop methods that allow understanding a processor. Because they can be simulated in any computer and arbitrarily perturbed, they are a great testbed" (p. 16). The earlier claim that our version is "the harder, open version" of their problem should credit them for proposing it. What remains new here is the ladder of heterogeneous systems, the neutral observation interface, and scoring against equivalence classes.
3. **Jonas & Kording 2015 found transistor types in one region, not "the structure of a microprocessor".** The arXiv abstract says it "automatically discovers the structure of a microprocessor"; the eLife abstract says it reveals "interesting structure" in it. The result is for a region containing the registers X, Y and S: types that "mirror the known structure", e.g. clocked transistors that retain state and a type that controls the registers' access to the data bus (`jonas2015automatic`, p. 11). Inputs are the wiring and the positions of the transistors.
4. **Moore's theorem is about finite experiments, not about equivalence classes.** Moore's second theorem, as quoted by Fields: "Given any machine S and any multiple experiment performed on S, there exist other machines experimentally distinguishable from S for which the original experiment would have had the same outcome" (Moore 1956, p. 140, via `fields2013bell`, p. 2). Finite observations put a lower bound on the size of the state space, never an upper bound. That belongs under limits of finite data (iteration 4 B.2), not under equivalence-class limits (B.1). The equivalence-class point has its own classic: Ashby (item B1 below).
5. **Locatello et al.'s impossibility is for i.i.d. observation.** Their setting is "where factors of variations are not observable at all, i.e. we only observe samples from P(x)" (`locatello2019challenging`, p. 3). It says nothing about a learner that intervenes, which is the proxy problem's setting. Hyvärinen & Pajunen's result is of the same kind: "if the space of mixing functions is not limited, there exists always an infinity of solutions"; with a conformal mixing in two dimensions the solution is unique up to a rotation (`hyvarinen1999nonlinear`, p. 2, OCR). Khemakhem et al. restore identifiability with an observed auxiliary variable (`khemakhem2020variational`, p. 3).
6. **Gold, read correctly, supports the access argument.** Gold's negative result is for positive data only (regular languages not identifiable from text; `peng2026language`, pp. 2 and 6). An experimenter who chooses inputs and sees outputs is closer to Gold's informant, for which richer classes are learnable in the limit (stated in secondary summaries; not checked against Gold's text). Peng, Saberi & Velegkas (ICLR 2026) prove that with perfect computational traces the recursively enumerable languages become identifiable in the limit (p. 1, Theorem 3.1 p. 13). The ICLR web abstract says "computable languages"; the PDF says recursively enumerable.
7. **The causal hierarchy is more specific than "passive data is not enough".** The Causal Hierarchy Theorem says the layers "almost never" collapse, in a measure-theoretic sense (`bareinboim2022pearl`, pp. 22 and 23). Ibeling & Icard's topological version concerns inferring counterfactuals from experimental or observational data (`ibeling2021topological`, pp. 1 and 2). Consequence for the testbed, my reading: a deterministic simulator that can be reset and replayed gives the experimenter the counterfactual layer directly (the same exogenous state, a different intervention), which no experiment on an animal can. The simulator is therefore more favorable than "unlimited probes" suggests, and a result in it does not transfer to a system that cannot be replayed.
8. **Meloux et al. is empirical, on Boolean functions and MLPs small enough to enumerate every candidate explanation** (`meloux2025everything`, p. 1). They also discuss dropping the demand for a unique explanation in favor of predictive or manipulability standards. Correct as stated in iteration 4.
9. **Goldblum et al. argue against the strong reading of no free lunch.** "Real-world problems disproportionately generate low-complexity data", neural networks share that preference, and one learner with a simplicity bias can serve "seemingly disparate problems" (`goldblum2024no`, p. 1). This is evidence *for* a general prior (simplicity) and against the claim that each system needs its own bias. Iteration 4 cited the paper only for the impossibility side.
10. **Hardware reverse engineering is supervised.** GNN-RE labels gates by sub-circuit class with GNNs trained on a generated, labelled dataset (`alrahis2022gnnre`, pp. 2 and 3); Gamora is multi-task node classification with labels (`wu2023gamora`, p. 4). Both work on the netlist, not on dynamics. Resolves the `[verify]` of iteration 3.
11. **Computational mechanics of CAs is partly by hand.** Domains are found "by inspection" in simple cases; complex patterns "require the automatic inference technique of ε-machine reconstruction" (`hanson1997computational`, p. 5). Resolves the "to check" of iteration 3.
12. **Small confirmations.** Weiss, Goldberg & Yahav (ICML 2018) is the L* paper; Clemens models six devices up to a 16550 UART (p. 3); Conmy et al. automate only the search for connections, with the behavior, metric and dataset chosen by the researcher (p. 1); Filan et al. define a module as "groups of neurons with strong internal connectivity but weak external connectivity" (p. 1); Vafa et al. 2024 is restricted to a reality "governed by a deterministic finite automaton" (p. 1); Vafa et al. 2025 report that orbital-trajectory models "consistently fail to apply Newtonian mechanics" on new tasks (p. 1).

### B. New evidence

**B1. A classic that states the problem (Lindy).** Ashby, *An Introduction to Cybernetics* (1956), chapter 6, "The Black Box" (`ashby1956introduction`). It poses the same problem, with the same examples:
- An engineer with a sealed box, inputs and outputs, "to deduce what he can of its contents"; the clinician who tests a patient with aphasia to deduce brain mechanisms (p. 86).
- "Let us make no assumptions at all about the nature of the Box and its contents, which might be something, say, that has just fallen from a Flying Saucer" (p. 87). This is the alien-artifact thought experiment, seventy years earlier.
- The questions: "What properties of the Box's contents are discoverable and what are fundamentally not discoverable?" and "What methods should be used if the Box is to be investigated efficiently?" (p. 87).
- The answer for a determinate box: the protocol of experiments identifies the mechanism "up to an isomorphism" and "cannot possibly be made to give more" (6/8, p. 94).
- The example: a spring, a wheel and a viscous trough are isomorphic to an inductance-resistance-capacitance circuit and to a second-order differential equation; "cover the central parts of the mechanism and the two machines are indistinguishable throughout an infinite number of tests" (pp. 95 and 96). This is the smoke test proposed in iteration 3 (do the oscillators of different substrates land together?).
- The digital computer "can be programmed to become isomorphic with any dynamic system whatever" (p. 96), which bears on Branch 1's question about a universal simulator.

**B2. A formal definition of reverse engineering, with an undecidability result.** Gurushankar & Grover, "A minimal intervention definition of reverse engineering a neural circuit" (arXiv 2110.00889, 2021), written in response to Jonas & Kording and Lazebnik. Their requirement: an agent that claims to have reverse-engineered a circuit must be able to give a minimal set of interventions that changes its input-output behavior to a desired one, under bounded rationality. Under assumptions on the model they show the goal is undecidable by reduction to Rice's theorem, and they give canonical systems where it can be done (p. 1). Iteration 4 said "a theorem that covers the whole proxy problem" was not found; this is a theorem for one formalization of it, and it must be read before that sentence is kept.

**B3. A counter-example with full access.** Marom et al. (2009): reverse engineering a neuro-system with a known mechanism, in a "static environment, primitive task and practically unlimited access to every piece of relevant information", gave "a perfectly valid but wrong induction of the system's design principle" (`marom2009precarious`, p. 1). This supports the equivalence-class warning with an experiment, and it is the strongest evidence against expecting success at the favorable end.

**B4. Prior art for the benchmark.**
- Geng, Chen, Arumugam & Griffiths (2025): LLMs reverse-engineering black-box programs, formal languages and equations from passive versus active data; passive observation plateaus below Bayesian inference, and intervening improves results (`geng2025large`, abstract). Direct evidence for the observation-regime point, at a small scale and with LLMs as learners.
- Gogleva et al., LUCA (arXiv 2611.03100, November 2026): a synthetic world with known ground truth, where agents learn unknown regulatory systems by budgeted experimentation, then predict interventions, diagnose faults and attempt rescues (Lazebnik's "fix it"). The authors' note on version 3 says its results "should not be relied upon pending correction"; the *design* is prior art, the numbers are not evidence.
- Haspel et al. (2023): argue that a complete state-dependent input-output description of C. elegans is now possible because every neuron can be perturbed and observed optically (`haspel2023time`, abstract).
- Brown (2014) retells the neuroscientists-and-computer parable (`brown2014tale`); a perspective, not evidence.

So the "not found" of iteration 3 B needs narrowing: benchmarks with known ground truth and budgeted experiments exist (LUCA, Geng et al.), with LLM agents and hand-given variables. Not found remains: neutral observations without the domain vocabulary, discovery of modules, and scoring against an equivalence class.

### C. What changes in the argument

- The question "what does it mean to understand a circuit?" has at least three formal answers in hand: up to isomorphism of the canonical representation (Ashby 1956), causal abstraction that commutes with interventions (Beckers & Halpern 2019; Geiger et al.), and minimal interventions that achieve a target behavior (Gurushankar & Grover 2021, Lazebnik's "fix it" made formal). The idea should choose among them or compare them, not invent a fourth.
- The impossibility results split into two families that the earlier text mixed: limits of finite or passive data (Moore, Gold on text, nonlinear ICA, Locatello, the causal hierarchy), which access can relax; and limits that access cannot relax (equivalence up to isomorphism, Ashby; non-unique explanations, Meloux et al. and Marom et al.; undecidability for unrestricted classes, Rice and Gurushankar & Grover).
- Three Epilogue sections already plan the philosophical side: *What Finite Data Cannot Fix* (Hume, Goodman, Kripke, no free lunch), *Does Structure Fix Meaning?* (Newman: structure only up to isomorphism, which is Ashby's result for machines) and *What a System Cannot Say About Itself* (Rice). The new paragraph should point to them and not restate them. Its own contribution is the access axis (passive, intervention, replay, trace) and the engineering classic (Ashby), which no section has.

## Iteration 6 (2026-11-17): a functional autoencoder with a contrastive stage

Proposed by the author after iteration 5; kept out of the written chapters, which stay with established results. Status: idea, a smoke test to design, not a claim.

### A. The design

The book already has a hand-built version: question 14 of `symbols_without_interior.qmd` (`rice_behavioural_embedding.py`) represents five programs by their outputs on probe inputs, so two sorting algorithms land together and `sort_unless_huge` lands with them because no probe reaches a list of 1,000 items. This design learns the behavior vector instead of fixing it, and moves from programs to physical systems.

1. **Systems.** Families in several substrates, built so that some members are isomorphic by construction (Ashby's spring, wheel and viscous trough against a series RLC circuit, 6/8), others agree only after linearization, and others share only a qualitative behavior. Chemistry enters through nonlinear oscillators (the Brusselator, say) matched to a nonlinear circuit (a van der Pol oscillator), since a deficiency-zero weakly reversible network cannot oscillate at all (idea 16). That gives a ladder: exact isomorphism, matched linearization, same qualitative behavior.
2. **Observation.** Each system shows only anonymous input and output channels, without names or units, with time and amplitude rescaled. Ashby matched L, R and C to the spring, the wheel and the friction "though not respectively"; the scaling decides whether a slow and a fast oscillator count as one function or two, and has to be chosen before the experiment.
3. **Stage 1, functional autoencoder, no labels.** An encoder reads a few probe experiments (an input signal and the response) and gives a code z; a decoder gets z and a new input and predicts the response. Good prediction means z holds the input-output map. The substrate is never given. Test: do isomorphic systems of different substrates get nearby codes without any pairing signal? This is the result that would say function is recoverable from behavior without supervision.
4. **Stage 2, contrast (InfoNCE, as in `embedding_spaces.qmd`).** Positives are the same function in two substrates; negatives are other functions. Because the pairs supply the equivalence, finding them together afterwards shows nothing. The test is transfer: pair only one family (oscillators), then ask whether families never paired (filters, integrators, a bistable switch) still meet across substrates. If they do, the model learned an invariance to substrate; if not, it learned a list of pairs.
5. **Hard negatives.** Pairs that agree under small inputs and differ under large ones, and pairs that differ only under an internal intervention: the `sort_unless_huge` trap for physical systems.
6. **Access as a variable.** Input-output probes only, then internal interventions, then replay. By Ashby, probes alone cannot separate isomorphic systems, so any further separation must appear only with more access.
7. **Baselines.** The hand-built behavior vector (raw responses to fixed probes, as in question 14) and a classical second-order transfer-function fit. A learned code that beats neither adds nothing at this scale.
8. **Scale.** A smoke test with numpy simulations of three or four families. It can falsify and illustrate; it cannot rank methods.

Reading of the outcomes: codes that meet without labels say function is recoverable from behavior up to equivalence; codes that meet only after pairing and do not transfer say the "meaning" was supplied by the pairs. Either is a finding. A pass says the code captures behavior up to equivalence, not purpose.

### B. Does the literature show this kind of architecture generalizing?

Checked on 2026-11-17 against abstracts and the pages named; all PDFs held (block "Ideas 16 and 17" of `references.bib`). Short answer: within a family of systems, yes; to related laws after a few examples, sometimes; zero-shot to unrelated laws, rarely and mostly as the authors' own claim; across substrates with contrastive pairing, nothing found.

| Kind of generalization | Evidence | Source and scope |
|---|---|---|
| New parameters of the same law | Yes. A context vector learned per environment conditions a dynamics model; new environments adapt fast, and the system parameters can be read from the context vectors "with minimal supervision". The authors' motivation states the baseline failure: data-driven models "fail to generalize to unseen systems that share the same general dynamics". | `kirchmeyer2022generalizing` (CoDA, ICML 2022), p. 1 |
| Zero-shot within a learned space of dynamics | Yes, inside the span. Function encoders represent a system as coefficients on learned basis functions, found by an inner product at run time; a larger amount of data is needed to learn the space than a single function. Nothing is claimed outside the span. | `ingebrand2024zeroshot`, pp. 1, 5 and 9 |
| Identification of the dynamics in latent space | Yes, for one system with guarantees. Contrastive learning identifies linear, switching linear and non-linear dynamics under a non-linear observation, with theoretical guarantees; symmetric encoders cannot identify non-trivial dynamics. This is the strongest support for stage 2 as system identification, but it is not transfer across systems. | `laiz2025selfsupervised` (ICLR 2025), pp. 1 and 8 |
| Across different physical laws, with adaptation | Partly. A graph network meta-trained on several Hamiltonian systems (mass-spring, pendulum, Hénon-Heiles, magnetic mirror) adapts with few examples to a held-out one and to further unseen systems (2D harmonic oscillator, Kepler). The shared prior is the Hamiltonian form, and adaptation uses a few target examples. | `song2024towards` (ICLR 2024), pp. 1, 8 and 14 |
| Across different laws, in context | Partly. A small transformer trained to forecast one dynamical system forecasts another without retraining, with an early trade-off between in- and out-of-distribution performance; the mechanism is a transfer-operator strategy (delay embedding, invariant sets). | `bao2026transformers` (preprint), p. 1 |
| New PDEs after pretraining | Authors' claim, with fine-tuning. Poseidon, pretrained on fluid dynamics, "generalizes very well to new physics that is not seen during pretraining" on 15 downstream tasks, fine-tuned with 1 to 128 task trajectories. | `herde2024poseidon`, pp. 1 and 59 |
| Prediction against recovery of the law | Against. Foundation models trained on orbits "consistently fail to apply Newtonian mechanics" on new tasks. | `vafa2025what`, p. 1 |
| Theory of out-of-distribution generalization | Against generalization without a commitment. From one training window the process that extrapolates correctly is not identifiable; a structural commitment (features, labels, model class) decides out-of-distribution behavior while leaving in-distribution performance unchanged. | `aguilar2026does` (NeurIPS 2026), p. 1 |
| Mapping between different physics | Found by search, not held: paired diffusion and wave fields mapped by neural operators, with a strong asymmetry in accuracy by direction. | Zhu, Lecompagnon & Ziegler, arXiv 2609.16853 (preprint) |

What this says for the design:

- Stage 1 is close to CoDA and function encoders, which work within a family. The new part is the substrate change, and Ashby's isomorphism says an ideal behavior encoder must put isomorphic systems together, since nothing in their behavior separates them. So within an isomorphism class the experiment tests the encoder, not a hypothesis about the world. The open question is the step between families.
- The step between families is where the evidence is thin: one positive line under a shared Hamiltonian prior with adaptation (Song & Jeong), one in-context result on small transformers (Bao et al.), one fine-tuned foundation model (Poseidon, authors' claim), and theory (Aguilar et al.) plus a negative result (Vafa et al.) that say the outcome is fixed by the inductive bias, not by the data. The stage 2 transfer test is therefore informative in both directions.
- No source found pairs systems across substrates contrastively and tests transfer to unpaired families. Search before claiming it: "contrastive" + "analogous systems" + "mechanical electrical"; "behavioral equivalence" + "representation learning" + "dynamical systems"; "function encoder" + "cross-domain"; bond graphs + learning.
